a tiny t-rex lives in your menu bar.

It runs faster as your Mac works harder. Click it and it tells you why: which app is on the CPU, the GPU, the disk and the battery, how many watts the whole machine is drawing, and what is keeping it awake.

Free and open source under the MIT license. Signed and notarized. macOS 14 Sonoma or later; every reading on Apple silicon, most on Intel.

click it.

A menu bar pet tells you that your Mac is busy. The dashboard behind the rex tells you what is making it busy, and what it was two minutes ago.

  • Which app is using the GPU

    Top processes ranked by CPU, memory, GPU, energy or disk, live or over the last 5 minutes to 24 hours, with helper processes folded into the app they belong to.

  • What was that spike?

    Click any graph to open the instant under the pointer: every process that ranked at that moment, all five measures side by side, plus wakeups a second. Arrow keys walk through time.

  • Who is keeping your Mac awake

    Every process holding a display or system sleep assertion, with the reason it gave.

  • Real watts

    Whole-machine power from the SMC, so fans, display and SSD count, plus CPU, GPU, Neural Engine and SoC figures, battery health and cycle count.

  • Every sensor

    CPU and GPU temperatures, fan speeds against their range, thermal pressure, and every individual sensor grouped by subsystem: 186 of them on an M1 Pro.

the 60-second tour.

sixteen rexes.

Pick one in Settings. Each speeds up with load, turns orange past 70% and red at 100%, and does something bigger past two-thirds. Every frame is drawn by code, not sprites.

    no helper. no sudo. no network.

    Everything is read through unprivileged system APIs. There is nothing to install alongside it, no password prompt, and the app never opens a network connection.

    At the default settings it costs about 1.4% of one core, measured on an M1 Pro with every core busy and the rex at full speed.

    Where the numbers come from
    CPU, per corehost_processor_info
    Memory and swaphost_statistics64, vm.swapusage
    GPU use, per appIOAccelerator statistics and accelerator clients in the IORegistry
    Temperatures, fans, system powerAppleSMC user client
    Component power and clocksIOReport energy counters
    Per-app CPU, memory, energy, disklibproc: proc_pidinfo, proc_pid_rusage
    Sleep assertionsIOPMCopyAssertionsByProcess

    get the rex.

    1. Download

      Get the notarized zip from GitHub Releases and unzip it.

    2. Move it to Applications

      Drag Rex Boing into Applications and open it. It asks for no permissions.

    3. Click the rex

      Click for the dashboard, right-click for the rex gallery and settings. Turn on Launch at login to keep it.

    Download for macOS
    brew install --cask rexyrex/tap/rex-boing

    Prefer to build it yourself? Clone the repository and run ./build.sh --install with Xcode 26 and XcodeGen.
